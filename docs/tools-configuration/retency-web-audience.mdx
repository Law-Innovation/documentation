This guide explains how to implement different privacy configurations for Retency Web Audience and how to reflect them in the Consenter Manager.

1. Configure the third-party service.
2. Map the selected configuration in the Consenter Manager.
3. Update your privacy policy to reflect the actual implementation.

The configurations are comparative examples, not guarantees of legal compliance. Verify that the guide matches your product edition, implementation, and current provider documentation.
</Callout>

## At a glance

**Scope.** Retency Web Audience JavaScript tag. Retency is a French company; data is hosted on managed servers in France operated by Ecritel.

**What the service does in this scope.** Retency Web Audience is designed exclusively for exempt audience measurement. It sets `_rwa_visitorid` (random 16-character first-party ID, 13 months) and `_rwa_optout` (13 months). Only the first three bytes of the IP address are kept; geolocation is limited to city level. No individual browsing data is kept longer than 24 hours; deduplicated statistics are kept over 18 months, and statistics are produced only with at least 10 contributions. The publisher is controller and Retency is processor. With opt-out, measurement is reduced to a simple page-view count without visit continuity or tracers.

**Configurations included.**

| Configuration | Summary |
|---|---|
| Configuration A — Lower risk | `optout: true` at initialisation for visitors who refuse: page-view count only, no tracers. |
| Configuration B — Medium risk (⚠ no-consent) | Default exempt measurement with `_rwa_visitorid`. |

> ⚠ **Recommendation — exemption is self-assessed and France-specific.** CNIL treats audience-measurement cookies as exempt from consent only where they serve solely the publisher's audience measurement, produce anonymous statistics, are not cross-referenced or passed on, and do not allow tracking across sites; it recommends a cookie lifetime of about 13 months without automatic extension and data retention of at most 25 months. CNIL now offers a self-evaluation tool rather than an approval programme, so describe the configuration as a **self-assessed exemption**. § 25 TDDDG in Germany contains no equivalent audience-measurement exemption. Run B without consent only after review by your DPO or independent counsel; otherwise use A for visitors without consent and B after consent.

## Step 1 — Configure Retency Web Audience

| # | Configuration area | Where to configure it | Configuration A — Lower risk | Configuration B — Medium risk (⚠ no-consent) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Processing purposes | Operator decision | Improve the service | Improve the service |
| 2 | Opt-out at initialisation | Tag constructor `optout: true` | `true` | `false` |
| 3 | User opt-out | `rwa.optout` → `_rwa_optout` (13 months) | — | Provide link |
| 4 | Personal data in tag fields | Do not pass emails or customer numbers | Required | Required |

## Step 2 — Map the configuration in the Consenter Manager

| Consenter Manager field | Configuration A — Lower risk | Configuration B — Medium risk (⚠ no-consent) |
|---|---|---|
| Processing purposes | Improve the service | Improve the service |
| Tracking method | No tracking | First party tracking (cross-session) |
| Identifier | No identifier | Device identifiers |
| Data categories | Aggregated site statistics, IP address anonymised | Aggregated site statistics, Browsing and interaction data, Device characteristics, Device identifiers, IP address anonymised, Non-precise location data |
| Legal role of data recipient | Processor | Processor |
| Personalisation model | No personalisation | No personalisation |
| Maximum storage duration | 18 months (statistics) | 13 months (cookie); 24 hours (individual data); 18 months (statistics) |
| Processing location | France | France |

## Privacy-policy follow-up

- Retency as processor; exempt measurement or reduced count; cookies; IP truncation; hosting in France; opt-out link.
